## All | Report: the European market is resetting
- After the COVID boom and the 2023–2025 oversupply, European sales are back at pre-pandemic levels. Heavy discounting hit mountain and urban bikes hardest.
- E-bikes in eight major markets (AT, DE, NL, FR, IT, UK, ES, CH): 4.07 million units in 2024 (down 5 %), worth €10.1 bn, average price €2,681.
- 2025 was fragmented: growth in the UK (first since 2020) and Belgium; stabilising in Germany, Spain and Austria; still under pressure in France, Switzerland and Italy; Netherlands down 7 %.
- Germany 2025: sales value €5.85 bn (down 7.7 %); online specialist retail at 23.8 % of sales and growing; workshop revenue up 13.5 %; stock levels expected to normalise by the end of 2026.
- France 2025: 1.84 million new bikes (down 6.2 %). UK 2025: market value up 5 % to just under £1.9 bn.
- Consolidation: YT Industries (insolvent 2025, relaunched) and Accell Group (Haibike, Ghost, Lapierre and others; insolvency since August 2026). Brand stability has become a buying factor.

## All | Trend: how riders buy and decide
- Riders buy online but still want sizing advice, test rides, assembly help and local service. Direct brands open stores and service points; dealer brands add online sales.
- After years of discounts, customers are price-aware and compare deals; independent tests are a key trust source (62 % of ENDURO readers say magazine tests decide their purchase).
- Many riders own several bikes and cross over: 93 % of Dutch gravel riders own another bike (more than half a road bike, 40 % a mountain bike); 54 % of Strava users record two or more sports.

## All | Rules: EU rules for marketing claims (general information, not legal advice)
- Green claims (Directive (EU) 2024/825, applies from 27 Sep 2026): generic claims such as "eco-friendly", "green" or "climate friendly" only with recognised excellent environmental performance; no claims about a whole product based on one aspect; no "climate neutral" claims based on offsetting; sustainability labels need certification or a public scheme; no false durability or repairability claims.
- Price reductions (Price Indication Directive, Art. 6a): any announced reduction must show the lowest price of the previous 30 days as the reference (CJEU C-330/23, 2024).
- Do not present legally required features (e.g. the 2-year legal guarantee) as a special selling point.
- Comparative advertising rules: see competitor_analysis.md.

## Road | Market: who buys road bikes
- Road is a mature, performance-driven segment. Buyers often already own a bike and upgrade; many also ride gravel (over half of Dutch gravel riders own a road bike).
- Price ladder in 2026 guides: budget alloy under £2,000 (e.g. Giant Contend SL); value carbon and aero at €6,000–7,500 (Cube Litening Aero, Giant Propel); superbikes at €12,000–15,000 (Specialized S-Works Aethos 2 about $14,000, Cervélo S5, Colnago Y1Rs, BMC Teammachine R 01).
- Direct-to-consumer brands put pressure on prices; reviewers reward fair pricing and penalise "hard to justify" price tags.
- Light, quiet e-road bikes are a growing niche (e.g. TQ-powered models).
- Sources: Cycling Weekly, "Best road bikes 2026" (26 Aug 2026), https://www.cyclingweekly.com/group-tests/best-road-bikes-461550 ; BikeRadar, "Best aero road bikes 2026" (18 Aug 2026), https://www.bikeradar.com/advice/buyers-guides/best-aero-road-bike

## Road | Trend: aero everywhere
- At the 2025 Tour de France, favourites rode aero bikes even on mountain stages. Modern aero bikes are light enough that the weight penalty barely matters; pro bikes sit just above the 6.8 kg UCI minimum.
- Categories blur: endurance bikes get race geometry and aero details; race bikes get more comfort.
- Integration: one-piece carbon cockpits, frame-specific bottle cages, faired computer mounts; Y-shaped cockpits (Colnago, Cervélo).
- New UCI rules since 1 Jan 2026: minimum 400 mm bar width at the outer edges, max 65 mm flare, max 65 mm rim depth in road races; shorter cranks (160–170 mm) are spreading from the pros.
- Disc brakes dominate; electronic shifting is mainstream at higher specs.
- Sources: TOUR (18 Oct 2026), link above ; Cycling Weekly (26 Aug 2026), link above

## Road | Trend: comfort, tyres and endurance bikes
- Tyre width growth has levelled off at 28–30 mm for racing; 28–35 mm clearance is common; tubeless is standard; tyres and rims are developed as a system.
- On rough roads the pros go wide: van der Poel won Paris–Roubaix 2024 on 32 mm tubeless tyres at about 3.5 bar; Pogačar used 38 mm at Roubaix.
- Endurance bikes are the "fast and comfortable" answer for most riders: race-inspired, wider tyres, more relaxed position.
- Sources: TOUR (18 Oct 2026), link above ; Cyclingnews, Paris–Roubaix winner's bike (7 Apr 2024), https://www.cyclingnews.com/features/the-paris-roubaix-winners-bike/

## Road | Explainer: what aero and tyres really do
- Air resistance is the biggest force on the flat: about 70 % of a rider's power goes into aerodynamics at 40 km/h (Cyclingnews wind-tunnel feature). Rider position matters most; frame, wheels and helmet add up.
- Benchmark: TOUR measured the Canyon Aeroad CFR frameset at 204 W to overcome its own air resistance at 45 km/h, an A grade; only the Cervélo S5 and Cannondale SystemSix kept up, and both are heavier.
- Aero vs climbing bike: in road.cc's lab test, a climbing bike was about 3 W more efficient in pedalling than its aero sibling on smooth and rough roads, so the difference is small; aero pays off at high speed and on flat or rolling routes.
- Tyres: at the same pressure, wider tyres roll slightly easier (GP5000: 32 mm 9.2 W vs 23 mm 10.0 W at 29 km/h); at the same comfort level, all widths roll about the same (11.3–11.5 W). The real gain of wider tyres is comfort and grip at lower pressure.
- Content use: honest, number-based explainers ("where your watts go") build trust; avoid overpromising aero gains for slower riders.

## Gravel | Market: who buys gravel bikes
- Gravel is the fastest-growing conventional segment in Germany and has overtaken youth bikes and ATB; retailers expect gravel and light e-bikes to grow most in 2026.
- Gravel riders spend more per bike: in a Dutch study, the average gravel bike cost €2,776 vs €1,517 for road and €1,086 for mountain bikes.
- They are experienced cyclists: 93 % own another bike; switching between road and gravel is popular; most ride 4–8 times a month or less; most join organised tours; just under half plan routes with Komoot or Strava.
- In the Netherlands, Canyon, Cannondale and Specialized dominate gravel; 61 % bought in a physical store, about a fifth from a pure online seller.

## Gravel | Trend: tyres, aero and storage
- Tyre clearance is the key differentiator: typically 40–58 mm, many new frames take 50 mm or more.
- Race models borrow road aero design (Ridley, Argon 18, Cervélo, BMC, Wilier, Canyon).
- Suspension forks, flexing frames and dropper posts appear more often; full-suspension gravel exists as a niche.
- 1x drivetrains and UDH dropouts are becoming standard; wide-range groupsets make the front derailleur unnecessary.
- Storage: down-tube compartments, bolt-on and integrated bags, mounts for bikepacking.
- Sources: BikeRadar, "Best gravel bikes 2026" (14 Oct 2026), https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/bikes/road-bikes/best-gravel-bikes ; TOUR (17 Sep 2026), link above

## Gravel | Explainer: tyre width and pressure
- Wider tyres let you run lower pressure for grip and comfort on loose ground; on rough surfaces, lower pressure keeps the bike from bouncing and losing speed.
- At equal comfort, tyre width barely changes rolling resistance on smooth surfaces (road test data: all widths about 11.4 W at the same tyre drop), so choose width for terrain and comfort.
- Canyon says many riders still run pressures meant for narrower tyres and offers a Pirelli-backed pressure tool in its app.
- Wider tyres cost little aero: in Canyon's wind tunnel, the Grail Gen 3 measured 110.0 W with 40 mm, 109.4 W with 45 mm and 113.6 W with 55 mm tyres at 35 km/h (manufacturer data).
- Content use: "wider is not slower" tips, pressure guides, mud vs dry tyre choice (reviewers often criticise stock tyres that only suit dry conditions).
- Sources: Bicycle Rolling Resistance, link above ; TOUR (17 Sep 2026), link above ; BikeRadar gravel (14 Oct 2026), link above

## MTB | Market: who buys mountain bikes
- Mountain bikes were hit hardest by oversupply and discounting; e-MTB is still the largest e-bike segment in Germany (38 % of e-bike sales in 2025).
- ENDURO reader survey 2025 (14,495 riders, 83 % in Europe): average age 44.5; average 3.1 bikes per rider; current bike about €4,600, next bike budget €5,300; 27 % of those planning a purchase consider an e-MTB; 18 % plan to buy within 12 months.

## MTB | Trend: trail is the sweet spot
- Most trail bikes have 120–150 mm rear travel (about 140 mm front / 130 mm rear as the balance point); a wave of lighter, playful 120–140 mm 29ers is expected.
- Adjustable geometry, flip chips, mullet options and in-frame storage are expected features; alloy bikes win on value.
- Trail and enduro blur; non-motorised enduro bikes are squeezed by e-enduro.

## MTB | Explainer: choosing travel and motor
- 120–130 mm: efficient, lively, for flowy trails and long days. 140–150 mm: the all-rounder for mixed and technical trails. 160 mm+: enduro, steep and rough terrain.
- Full-power e-MTB: more climbs and laps per ride, heavier. Light e-MTB: rides closer to a normal bike, less range.
- Handling is what buyers value most, so content should describe how a bike feels on the trail, not only list specs.
